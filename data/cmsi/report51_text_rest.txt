one-third of these graduates actually obtains their third officer/fourth engineer certificates.59 This
suggests that graduates do not consider the merchant marine to be a relatively desirable or
competitive career path. Other surveys reinforce this trend. Surveys tracking the rate of Dalian
Maritime University graduates with maritime majors choosing to work aboard ships found a strong
decline since 2000, with only 50 percent choosing to work at sea within five years after graduating.
Many graduates entering the workforce also leave soon after. One COSCO Shipping sampling
analysis indicated that the attrition rate of fresh graduates within one year was as high as 30%, and
65% after five years.60 Poor job retention after graduation could result in serious shortages of future
officers as older mariners leave the workforce.
The merchant marine is also losing numbers as some personnel choose not to return to work. This
trend has gradually worsened with younger generations that join the merchant marine and find less
fulfillment in the career and less attraction to the pay.61 Analysis and discussion of this in China
covers a broad range of reasons for personnel weighing whether to leave the merchant marine. These
include the hardships of shipboard work and life, such as isolation, diet, and the prison-like
environment of the ship. The vessel living conditions of major shipping companies are generally
tolerable, but conditions aboard many coastal route vessels are often considered deplorable. The
labor marketplace also contains many intermediary agencies with bad practices, sometimes resulting
in delays in salary payout and other issues. Atop all these conditions, work at sea is inherently
dangerous, naturally causing many seafarers to carefully consider whether work conditions are worth
the personal risk. This risk is worsened by safety concerns over reduced crewing and overworked
crews. 62

Merchant marine pay is not as competitive compared to shore-based jobs as it once was. China does
not maintain official data on seafarer pay, but it can be found in industry survey data. One assessment
found that pay significantly declined for operations-grade personnel from 2010-2017, likely due to
seafarer oversupply.65 The following few years saw stagnating or declining pay for most ship

and chemical industries are somewhat more stable and require more specialized certifications for
seafarers, resulting in steady pay increases over time. Bulk cargo lines saw the lowest increases in

Seafarer Development Report, which concluded that it will be difficult for the workforce to meet the
needs of the PRC’s shipping development.67
The COVID-19 Pandemic wrought massive disruption to the global seafarer market. During the
pandemic’s height, hundreds of thousands of seafarers were stranded and unable to get relief and
repatriation due to travel restrictions. Many stranded seafarers experienced fatigue, fear, and mental
health problems during this period, stuck at sea and unable to go ashore. Those who were able to get
relieved still had problems returning home and faced several weeks of quarantine, with newly
mutating strains of COVID-19 causing even further complications.68 Restrictions were particularly
strict in China, which implemented a zero-COVID policy that entailed mass testing, mandatory
quarantine, and limits on movement.69

Managing the Seafarer Workforce—the Maritime Safety Administration (MSA)
The MSA, under the Ministry of Transport, is the core government organ for administering China’s
merchant marine. It is responsible for managing waterway safety, vessel registrations and
inspections, prevention of ship pollution, and administering the examination, credentialing, and
supervision of the merchant marine.76 The MSA is primarily an administrative and law enforcement
government organ, separate from the China Coast Guard (中国海警), which is a law enforcement
organization chiefly responsible for maritime rights protection and law enforcement under the
Central Military Commission’s military chain of command.77 This separation of responsibilities
relieves the Coast Guard of the heavy burden of administering China’s massive merchant fleet and
workforce.

Merchant Marine Training, Examination, and Credentialing
The Ministry of Transport promulgates the “Rules for the Examination and Licensing of Seafarers of
the People’s Republic of China” (中华人民共和国海船船员适任考试和发证规则) (referred to in
shorthand as “The 20 Rules” / “20规则”), which stipulates the rules governing merchant marine
licensing and qualifications. It is periodically updated to reflect industry and regulatory imperatives,
with the most recent version updated in July 2020 and amended in April 2022.85 This section will
explore this credentialing system in greater detail as all PRC seafarers live, work, and progress in
their careers according to these rules. Much like the rest of the world’s merchant marine, Chinese
seafarers navigate a complex system of certification based on their qualifications, training, sea time,
and the classes of vessels they serve aboard.
PRC merchant mariners are licensed according to the navigational areas in which they work and the
vessels on which they serve. Navigational zones, as mentioned previously, are divided into unlimited
navigation, coastal navigation, and inland waterways. This section will focus exclusively on seafarer
licensing for unlimited and coastal navigation zones.86 Rules for “inland waterways” are covered
under separate regulations.87

requirements for a raise in grade (职务晋升) . There are separate career tracks for the deck and engine
departments, each requiring similar basic safety and professional skills proficiency training and
different job-specific proficiency training (岗位适任培训 ). An extensive detailing of specific
requirements in training, course hours, examination, and expectations is contained in the “Seagoing
Vessel Mariner Training Outline” (海船船员培训大纲) , published by the Ministry of Transport. This
643-page document covers training requirements for every ship position and vessel type-specific
certificate of competency.91
There are also several modifications that can be made to a mariner’s license, termed in the United
States “an increase in scope.” This entails the removal of limitations to an existing license, such as
expanding the navigational area (航区扩大) and increasing tonnage (吨位提高) or horsepower (功率
提高) limits of a license.92 Mariners will need to satisfy time in position, basic and job-specific
training, and English exam requirements; take the relevant license modification exam; and satisfy
additional training requirements across different subjects depending on the modification for which
they are applying (tonnage or navigational area).93 Seafarers applying to work on international routes
must pass the “Professional English Test for Seafarers on International Voyages” and achieve a 60
percent score on the listening and oral portions.94 See the Appendix for a description of application
requirements for training, qualifications, and examinations for seafarer certificates of competency.

Joining the Merchant Marine
Aspiring seafarers can begin their careers from the bottom as one of the ratings or attend one of the
many maritime schools to become a licensed officer. Some rise through the ranks without attending a
school, referred to in the United States as a “hawsepiper” or in China simply as those that did not
attend the academy.97
To join as a rating on coastal and international routes, one must be between the ages of 18 and 60,
pass the physical health exam, complete basic safety training and pass the corresponding exam, and
complete any required professional skills training.98 Basic safety training is often referred to as the
“Four Certificates“ (四小证), a safety training course that all seafarers must pass, including four
training sub-courses: personal survival, fire prevention and fighting, elementary first aid, and
personal safety and social responsibility.99 After completing these requirements, seafarers receive a
“seafarer certificate of competency” (海船船员适任证书) and a “Seaman’s Identity Document” (船
员服务簿), within which their sea time will be recorded in this book and used for license upgrades.
They may then serve in unlimited and coastal navigational areas. Additional training, service time,
and good performance and safety record are required to qualify for a RFPNW.100
Ratings applying to become third officer or fourth engineer must complete no less than 18 months of
sea time, complete the required job-specific training, and then pass the examination. Additionally,
they should have performed bridge or engine room watch duties for no less than six months on a
vessel of corresponding class under the guidance of the captain or a qualified officer as part of their
on-board training (船上见习 ). Job-specific training courses, as determined by the MSA, include
subjects on navigation, ship maneuvering and collision avoidance, ship management, ship structure
and cargo transport, and maritime English. They will also need to complete practical assessments in
“International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers (STCW),” International
Maritime Organization, Undated, https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/humanelement/pages/stcw-conv-link.aspx.

Ascending the ranks from a rating to captain can theoretically be done in eight to ten years given no
wasted time and smooth promotions. In reality, it usually takes much longer than ten years.102
Seafarers must meet sea time requirements within proper vessel class, tonnage, and navigation areas,
and then complete the administrative processes of training, examinations, and other steps to ensure
they achieve their promotions. In many cases, individuals will experience delays, mistakes, or lack of
opportunity depending on market conditions, requiring well over a decade to achieve the rank of
captain.103
The more common route to becoming a seafarer officer is through the PRC’s various maritime
academies and institutions. Generally, high school graduates will enroll in these institutions as
maritime majors (航海类专业 ). Eligibility to participate in the seafarer competency examination
requires at least two years of full-time maritime secondary vocational education or above; or, for
those who completed full-time non-maritime education at a junior college or above, completion of at
least 12 months of training for the positions of third officer or fourth engineer. Upon completion of
their courses, candidates may apply to take the unlimited navigation third officer / fourth engineer
exams. A select few universities offering excellent undergraduate degrees in maritime majors are
approved by the MSA to allow their graduates to apply for second officer / second engineer
qualification examinations.104

Sea time gained in other maritime fields can be used to advance in China’s merchant marine or aid in
upgrading to different navigational zones or more advanced licenses. According to article 23 of the
“Rules of the People’s Republic of China of Seafarer Examination and Certification,” personnel who
have served on inland waterway vessels, ocean-going fishing vessels, or military vessels may apply
for corresponding certificates of competency for seafarers. The highest position for which they may
apply is chief officer or second engineer, their experience must be of compatible relevant sea service
with good performance records, and they must participate in the required training and pass the exams
required for the position for which they are applying.121 Some may be granted exemption from job-
specific training and may directly apply for competency certificate exams and, upon completion of a
traineeship, apply to be issued such certificates. These include demobilized personnel on military
vessels who have received a post-secondary degree in a navigation major or above from a full-time
maritime academy, or a post-secondary degree in a related field such as navigation or marine
engineering from a military academy. It also includes personnel with service aboard military vessels
as sailors or engineers that are applying for RFPNW or RFPEW certificates.122

Fraud and Abuse
In practice, the qualification and certification process for mariners involves many cases of fraud and
abuse. Some mariners seeking to ascend the ranks for better positions on vessels or within companies
may undertake illegal practices in falsifying time served at sea in their Seaman’s Identity Document.
Other issues involve cheating on exams, sharing certificates for personal profit, scams from fake
seafarer services, company’s attempting to skirt minimum manning requirements, and many other
issues that not just break the law, but also create hidden maritime safety dangers in shipping. These
are longstanding issues and the MSA has perpetually sought to crack down on bad actors and
promote the industry and seafarer profession.133

reported sea time, qualifications, and other issues. According to Guangxi-based MSA experts, some
of the common falsification methods include fake endorsements in Seafarer Identity Documents and
falsifying crew member information in the ship’s port entry and departure reporting system. Captains
might record false sea service for crew members, often due to company requests, “favors” (人情), or
for personal profit, regardless of whether they were present aboard the vessel. While also falsifying

Crew Service Agencies
Numerous crew service agencies (船员服务机构 ) operating across China provide services to
Chinese seafarers. According to regulations on the administration of seafarer services, these are
commercial entities “that act as an agent for mariners to handle relevant procedures such as applying
for training, examinations, and obtaining certificates (including foreign crew certificates)” as well as
businesses that “act as an agent for crew employers to manage crew affairs and provide manning for
ships.” These agencies are administered and licensed by the MSA.141 There are also numerous
seafarer dispatch agencies (海员外派机构) licensed to provide manning and crew services to
seafarers working on foreign-flagged vessels, subject to separate regulations.142 Crew service
agencies provide important services for seafarers navigating the complex experience, training, and
qualification requirements for their professions, as well as providing ship management services for
shipping enterprises, maintaining crew member files, and monitoring their status.143
Regulations state crew service agencies must meet certain standards to provide these services and not
engage in illegal activities such as bribery, forgery, acting on behalf of non-qualified crew service
agencies, and many other illegal activities.144 The MSA may impose a fine of no less than 30,000 and
no more than 150,000 RMB for agencies committing fraud, and in serious cases can suspend the
agency’s services for up to two years.145 Despite the threat of fines and suspension, the industry is
rife with illegal and fraudulent actors that cheat seafarers out of millions in earnings. They are further
enabled by high levels of youth unemployment and desperate economic conditions, especially for
people from poorer regions. Market conditions with periods of oversupply in seafarer labor worsen
